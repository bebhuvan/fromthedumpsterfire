Signatures

Seven small things an editor at a desk would do to a page, each used in one place and meaning one thing. The aim is the old web's spirit (a person's hand everywhere, nothing a template) in our own language, not its costume (no counters, marquees or glitter). Pick any; none depends on another.

1

The ledger

At the foot of every item, an honest account of whose words are on the page, counted by code from the bundle: the editor's in his italic, the source's in the serif, the machine's in blue. The old web's hit counter, turned into the house's promise.

The date stamp

The cover's date as a library stamp in the Haul's ink, a few degrees off true: SALVAGED · 8 Nov 2026. A correction would add a second stamp, CORRECTED, with its version. Once per cover, nowhere else.

3

The page that burned

The 404 ("Nothing here, or it burned") set on a sheet whose corner has actually burned away, its edge charred in ember. The one page where the fire is literal; the old web's 404s were where sites were most themselves.

4

Taped plates

The public-domain plates, the pictures salvaged from archives, held to the page with two strips of tape, as a scrapbook holds a print. Only plates; a chart or a picture block stays clean.

5

An ornament per edition

Each Haul gets its own printer's ornament in its ink, set between the field note's sections and at the end of each item (beside the flame), drawn as marks like the flame. Old printers had their fleurons; old sites had their dingbats. A reader comes to know an issue by its mark.

The slug and the neighbours

The footer as a printer's slug (what it is set in, when it was last salvaged, whose hand) and a neighbours line: the editor's other places and a few sites he salvages from, the old web's blogroll and webring. The list would be his.
