As We May Think

Vannevar Bush, The Atlantic, July 1945

Everyone cites the memex and almost nobody reads the essay. Read it now, while generated text piles up around us, and it stops being a period piece. Bush wasn't worried about storage. He was worried about finding the one thing that mattered in a pile that kept growing. B.

The summation of human experience is being expanded at a prodigious rate, and the means we use for threading through the consequent maze to the momentarily important item is the same as was used in the days of square-rigged ships.

Vannevar Bush, As We May Think, section 1

Read the essay at theatlantic.com · 40 minutes

Note Published in July 1945, while Bush still ran the wartime Office of Scientific Research and Development. The war was ending, and he was asking what scientists should turn to next. Wikipedia: Vannevar Bush

Note Douglas Engelbart read it as a young Navy radar technician in the Philippines. Two decades later he gave the demonstration that introduced the mouse and hypertext. Wikipedia: Douglas Engelbart
