# Charts

The chart kit, built 2026-10-25 (brief: `docs/CHARTS-BRIEF.md`, report: `docs/CHARTS-REPORT.md`).
A chart is a block on an item, like a pull quote or a picture. The editor brings the numbers and
writes the title and the note; code draws the chart in the house style, the same on the website and in
the app, light and dark. A chart-led item has its own content type, **Chart**, printed as **the
figure**.

Everything a reader sees on a chart traces to rows stored in the bundle. The website draws the SVG
from those rows on every render. The app shows two pictures (light and dark) drawn from the same
scene, because it has no SVG runtime. The rows also print as a table under every chart.

Comps with real data: `docs/design-comps/charts/` (`time.html`, `compare.html`, `spread.html`,
`weather.html`; screenshots in `shots/`, light and dark, 390 and 1280 wide).

## The forms

Sixteen forms. Each draws at two widths: the **wide** figure (720 px, the reading column and some of
the air, from 560 px of column) and the **phone** figure (375 px, which is also the app's picture). A
container query on the figure's own width picks which one shows; both are in the page.

| Form | For | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `line` | change over time | Lines labelled at their ends, no legend. One loud line in the edition ink among quiet grey ones (`options.focus`), or up to six in the categorical order. `lo`/`hi` draw the uncertainty as a wash. A missing year breaks the line. |
| `multiples` | one line per place or group | A grid of panels on one shared scale (ticks on the first panel of each row), every other facet faint behind each panel, the focus facet in ink. Four across wide, two on a phone. |
| `area` | parts of a whole over time | Stacked from zero, 2 px ground gaps between layers, parts named in their band or beside it. At most six parts. A missing value is refused (it would shift every layer above). |
| `bar` | amounts | Horizontal bars for categories (`label`), columns for years (`x`). Square ends, at most 24 px thick, always from an ink zero line. Values at the tips; columns label only the last, the largest, a negative minimum and the focus. `lo`/`hi` draw whiskers; `n` under 30 draws hollow. |
| `dot` | values where zero means nothing | Cleveland dot plot with a hairline guide per row; several series get a key; whiskers for `lo`/`hi`; hollow for small samples. |
| `dumbbell` | two values per category | The first value a ring, the second a solid dot, so they read apart without a second hue. |
| `slope` | change between two moments | Two posts headed by the column names; values spread so labels never touch. |
| `scatter` | two measures together | Dots ringed in the ground colour; `size` is dot area; named points labelled without collisions; `scale.x.log`/`scale.y.log` labelled. |
| `bump` | rankings over time | Ranks worked out by code in each period among the series shown (ties broken by name), names at both ends. |
| `stripes` | one value a year (Ed Hawkins's warming stripes) | Diverging ramp around a stated midpoint, with a key. |
| `band` | a year of daily weather | After the New York Times weather chart: record range, normal range, the year's daily low to high in the edition ink, record days marked, the year's highest and lowest named, a key drawn as one sample day. Built from daily data by `bandSummary` (below). |
| `calendar` | a value for every day | Weeks as columns, days as rows, months named; a missing day is an empty outlined square. |
| `heatmap` | a value for each pair of categories | Month by year, say; 2 px gaps; missing cells outlined; a ramp key. |
| `ridgeline` | how a distribution shifts across groups | A Gaussian kernel density per group (Silverman's bandwidth), one shared height scale, a median tick, every value marked for groups under 30. |
| `beeswarm` | every value as a dot | Deterministic packing (no randomness), groups as rows, each group's median marked, named dots labelled. |
| `waffle` | parts of a whole (instead of a pie) | 10 × 10 squares by largest-remainder rounding (or `options.per`). Past five parts the smallest fold into a quiet "Other" block, each still listed with its own value. |

There are no pies, donuts, 3D charts or dual-axis charts; the validator refuses each by name and says
what to use instead.

**Annotations** are a layer of every form that has room for them: `point` (a callout tied to a row,
placed clear of the data with a short leader), `period` (a shaded span with its name, such as a war or
a lockdown) and `rule` (a reference line with its label, such as the Paris goal). The editor's
annotations are set in the italic serif; a machine annotation would be in the blue sans and is checked
(below).

## Design

Typography carries it, as everywhere in Salvage. Around the plot, on the website and in the app:

- **Figure n** in quiet sans with the number in ember (a page citing its figures, as the cover route
  cites items), then the editor's **title** in Newsreader Display and the **note** in the italic
  serif.
- The **units line** in quiet sans: what the numbers are, where and when ("°C, difference from the
  1861–1890 average · World, 1850 to 2025"). Built by code from the editor's fields (`unitsLine`).
- The plot.
- The machine's **glosses** in the blue sans (`chartGlosses`): the window shown against the source's
  span, log scales, a scale that does not start at zero, missing values, what the whiskers or the wash
  are, small samples, the quiet lines, how ranks or squares were worked out, and the `method` of
  derived rows.
- The **credit**: each maker and dataset linked, "via" the redistributor, the licences as stated, and
  the day the data was fetched.
- **The numbers**: the data table (a `<details>` on the website, a tappable row in the app).

Inside the plot: hairline gridlines in the rule colour, tick labels in ink2 sans in the left margin
with the unit on the top tick, an ink line at zero, square-ended marks, 2 px ground gaps between
touching marks and a 2 px ground ring on dots. Text never wears a data colour. One loud thing per
chart: the focus series in the **Haul's edition ink**, everything else quiet. Ember is never data.
Machine blue is never data.

**Chart tokens** (`chartInk` in `tokens.ts`). Each was run through the dataviz palette validator
(OKLCH lightness band, chroma floor, protan/deutan separation, normal-vision floor, contrast) on this

## The data spec

A `Chart` (`types.ts`) is JSON:

```json
{
  "form": "line",
  "title": "The world has warmed by about one and a half degrees",
  "note": "The shaded band is the 95 per cent range of the estimate.",
  "units": "°C",
  "geography": "World",
  "span": { "from": "1850", "to": "2025" },
  "sourceSpan": { "from": "1850", "to": "2026" },
  "sources": [{
    "maker": "Met Office Hadley Centre", "dataset": "HadCRUT5",
    "url": "https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/hadobs/hadcrut5/",
    "licence": "Open Government Licence v3", "retrieved": "2026-10-25",
    "via": { "name": "Our World in Data", "url": "https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/temperature-anomaly", "licence": "CC BY 4.0" }
  }],
  "measure": { "basis": "anomaly", "base": "the 1861–1890 average" },
  "data": {
    "columns": [
      { "key": "year", "label": "Year", "type": "year" },
      { "key": "anomaly", "label": "Global average temperature anomaly", "type": "number" },
      { "key": "lower", "label": "Lower bound (95% confidence)", "type": "number" },
      { "key": "upper", "label": "Upper bound (95% confidence)", "type": "number" }
    ],
    "rows": [[1850, -0.05, -0.23, 0.13], [1851, 0.1, -0.08, 0.28]]
  },
  "encode": { "x": "year", "y": "anomaly", "lo": "lower", "hi": "upper" },
  "options": { "focus": [] },
  "annotations": [{ "kind": "period", "from": 1914, "to": 1918, "text": "First World War", "by": "editor" }]
}
```

- **Columns** are `number`, `text`, `year` (a whole number) or `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD` or `YYYY-MM`). A
  missing value is `null`, never text or NaN.
- **Encode** maps roles to columns: `x`, `y`, `series`, `label`, `facet`, `lo`, `hi`, `n`, `size`,
  `from`, `to`, and the band's `normalLo`, `normalHi`, `recordLo`, `recordHi`. `FORMS[form]` lists
  each form's required and optional roles and the column types each role takes.
- **Measure** says what the numbers are: `absolute`, `per-capita`, `share` or `rate` (with a
  `denominator`), `index`, `anomaly` or `change` (with a `base`), and `money` (`currency`, `nominal` or
  `real`, `baseYear` for real).
- **Options**: `colour` (`focus`, `categorical`, `sequential`, `diverging`; each form allows some),
  `focus` (names to set loud), `midpoint` (`{ value, label }`, diverging only), `sort` (`value` or
  `given`), `per` (waffle), `rank` (bump).
- **Scale**: `y.log`, `x.log` (scatter), `y.zero`.
- **method**: one plain sentence when code derived the rows (a band's normals, monthly means). It
  prints as a gloss.

In a bundle a chart is `{ "kind": "chart", "chart": { … }, "images": { "light": {url, width, height}, "dark": {…} } }`.

## The validator's rules

`validateChart(chart)` runs on the Desk before a chart can be kept (and live in the editor), again at
publish, and inside `validateHaul` before a bundle renders. Each rule has a test in
`packages/shared/src/charts/validate.test.ts`; the comps fixtures must pass it too.

| Rule | What it requires |
|---|---|
| `title`, `units`, `geography` | A title; what the numbers are measured in; where the data is about, or `null` for no place. |
| `source`, `licence` | At least one source with maker, dataset, an https link, the retrieved date, and the licence as the institution states it ("unknown" and empty are refused). A redistributor (`via`) needs its own licence. |
| `zero-baseline`, `log` | Bars, areas and waffles start at zero and are never on a log scale. A line may leave zero out; a gloss then says so. A log scale needs every value above zero, only on lines, dots and scatters, and is always labelled. |
| `dual-axis`, `3d`, `pie`, `colour`, `unknown-field` | No second y axis, no 3D, no pies (use a waffle or bars), no home-made palettes or rainbows. Any field the spec does not know is refused, so nothing unchecked reaches a bundle. |
| `colour`, `too-many-series` | Ordered values (numbers, years, dates) never get separate hues. Categorical colour is capped at six series (twelve parts for a waffle, which folds the rest); past that, make one loud or use small multiples. |
| `midpoint` | A diverging ramp needs a meaningful midpoint with its name; a midpoint outside the data is warned. |
| `basis`, `denominator`, `base`, `index-base`, `money` | Per-capita or absolute is stated; shares and rates show their denominator; an index states its base and the series must read that level in that year ("2010 = 100"); anomalies and changes state their base; money states its currency and nominal or real, real money its base year; nominal money over ten years or more is warned. |
| `uncertainty`, `sample-size` | If the data carries uncertainty (lower/upper bounds, confidence, standard errors) or sample sizes, they must be encoded and shown, or the column removed. Bounds come in pairs, in order, around the value. Samples under 30 are drawn hollow. |
| `missing` | Missing values are `null` and drawn as missing (gaps, empty squares, "no data"); absent years in a yearly series are found and drawn as gaps too. A stacked area with a hole is refused. |
| `span` | A chart over time records the span it shows (which must be exactly the rows' extent) and the full span of the source. A narrower window gets a gloss: "Shown from 2001 to 2005; the source runs from 1871 to 2025." |
| `data`, `size`, `duplicate`, `encode` | Every cell matches its column type (text cells and column names at most 200 characters); at most 20,000 rows, 80,000 cells and 1.5 MB in all (a chart is one D1 value); malformed tables are refused, never crashed on; no two points at the same place; every role points at a column of the right type. |
| `figures` | Every figure in a **machine** annotation must be a number in the rows (rounded no finer than it is written), a year in them, or a number in the editor's own stated metadata. Fail-closed. A machine rule must sit at a value in the data. Numbers written as words ("doubled", "five") are not detected yet. The editor's own figures that are not in the data only raise a warning. |
| `style:*` | Machine annotations, glosses, the method and the text alternative pass `checkStyle`. |

What code writes from the rows (glosses, the description, ranks, waffle squares, densities, band
normals) is computed, not drafted, and is tested; no model writes any number on a chart. Nothing in
this kit calls a model. If model-drafted annotations are added later, they come in as `by: 'machine'`
and the `figures` and `style` rules above already gate them.

## Getting the data in

### A table (CSV or TSV)

Paste or upload on the Desk, or `npm run editorial -- chart-csv FILE`. `csvToData` reads the header,
types each column (dates, years, numbers, text), reads thousands commas and any minus sign, and
treats blank, `NA`, `n/a`, `..`, `-` as missing. A cell is a number only when the whole cell is one;
anything else is reported as a problem, never coerced. The editor states the source, its licence and
the source's full span.

### Our World in Data (checked 2026-10-25)

- Endpoints (Chart API, docs.owid.io/projects/etl/api/chart-api/, "License: CC BY 4.0"):
  `https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/<slug>.csv?v=1&csvType=full&useColumnShortNames=true` (columns
  `entity, code, year|day, …`) and `<slug>.metadata.json` (each column's short name, unit, timespan and
  a `fullMetadata` link).
- Licences: each indicator's `https://api.ourworldindata.org/v1/indicators/<id>.metadata.json` lists its
  `origins` with `producer`, `title`, `urlMain` and `license { name, url }`. The adapter credits each
  producer with its own licence and Our World in Data as the redistributor (CC BY 4.0: "Data produced
  by us falls under our permissive CC BY license … provided that you cite us", ourworldindata.org/faqs).
  An origin without a stated licence is refused. Example: HadCRUT5 is "Open Government License v3".
- The adapter keeps the places the editor chooses (a chart over 20,000 rows returns its list of places
  first) and an optional year range, and records the full span of the source.
- A year at the end of OWID's span can be partial (the temperature series runs to 2026 in October
  2026). The comps drop it and the window gloss says so; check the latest year before keeping a chart.

### World Bank (checked 2026-10-25)

- API v2: `https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/<id>?format=json` (name, source), then
  `https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/<ISO;ISO>/indicator/<id>?format=json&per_page=20000`.
- Licence: `https://api.worldbank.org/v2/sources/<source>/series/<id>/metadata?format=json` carries
  `License_Type` ("CC BY-4.0" for World Development Indicators) and `License_URL`
  (datacatalog.worldbank.org/public-licenses: "licenses datasets under the Creative Commons Attribution
  4.0 International license (CC-BY 4.0). Many datasets are available under other licenses. They are
  labeled accordingly"). A series without `License_Type` is refused.
- Units and money come from the indicator's name: "(constant 2015 US$)" becomes real US dollars at
  2015 prices, "(current US$)" nominal, "% of GDP" a share of GDP, "per capita" per person.
- Only yearly series for now.

### Other sources

Any source works through the table route, as long as its licence is stated. The comps use Open-Meteo's
historical API (ERA5 reanalysis) for Delhi's daily temperatures: CC BY 4.0, with a link to
Open-Meteo.com next to the data, and the free API is for non-commercial use
(`docs/design-comps/charts/raw/SOURCES-B.md`). NOAA, IMD, RBI and data.gov.in (Government Open Data
License, India) data come in the same way.

### A temperature band from daily data

`bandSummary(daily, { year, normalFrom, normalTo })` turns daily highs and lows into the band's rows:
that year's low and high each day, the normal range (the mean daily low and mean daily high for that
calendar day over the normal years, 29 February over the leap years only, missing when fewer than half
the years have a value), and the records (the lowest low and highest high for that calendar day up to
and including the year). `bandMethod` writes the sentence that becomes the chart's `method`.

### Someone else's chart, as it stands

When the editor wants the original picture (an Our World in Data chart, a newspaper's figure), it is a
**picture block**, under the picture credit rule: maker, holder, licence as stated, a link back. Give
the Desk the image link (Our World in Data serves `https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/<slug>.png`); it
is fetched through `guardedFetch` with a byte cap, kept in MEDIA and copied to FILES at publish, never
hotlinked. Redrawing from the data is better whenever the licence allows: it is ours, it is
accessible, and the numbers are in the table.

## How the editor adds a chart

### On the Desk

1. On the item (a Chart item for a chart-led find, or any item for a chart that goes with it), choose
   **Chart** under Add.
2. Bring the numbers: paste a table or upload a CSV; or paste an Our World in Data link (choose places
   and years, fetch again); or give a World Bank indicator code and country codes.
3. Pick a form (only the forms the columns can feed are offered), check which column plays which role.
4. Write the title and the note in your own words, the units, and the place (or "about no place").
5. Say what the numbers are (absolute, per person, share or rate of what, index or anomaly from what,
   money nominal or real), and for a table the source, its licence and the source's full span.
6. Optional: which series is loud, colour, midpoint, order, log, callouts, periods and lines.
7. The preview redraws as you go (the wide figure and the phone figure, which is the app's picture),
   with the units line, the glosses and the validator's errors and warnings. **Keep** is off until the
   errors are gone; the server checks again.
8. At **Publish**, the Desk draws each chart's light and dark pictures for the app in your browser
   ("Drawing the charts for the app…") and sends them with the publish.

The source's own title and notes show beside the editor as reference, never in the editor's fields.

### From the terminal (or an agent)

```sh
npm run editorial -- chart-fetch --data source.json      # {"provider":"owid","chart":URL,"entities":["India","World"]}
npm run editorial -- chart-csv rainfall.csv              # a table as chart data (local)
npm run editorial -- chart-check --data chart.json       # the validator, units line, glosses (local)
npm run editorial -- chart-draw /tmp/look --data chart.json   # PNGs to look at (local)
npm run editorial -- chart it_ITEM --data chart.json     # keep it on an item (the Desk checks it)
npm run editorial -- chart-edit bk_BLOCK --data chart.json    # replace a kept chart
npm run editorial -- publish hl_HAUL --confirm-publish   # draws the app pictures, then publishes
```

`publish` fetches the preview, draws each chart's pictures here with resvg and the app's font cuts
(`scripts/editorial/charts.mjs`), and sends them with the publish, exactly as the Desk does.

## The app's pictures

The app has no SVG runtime and stays lean, so it shows pictures of the phone figure. Drawing them on
the server is ruled out by the Workers free plan's CPU limit (as for share cards), so the editor's
side draws them at publish: the Desk in the browser, the terminal in Node, both with
`@resvg/resvg-wasm` and the app's own TTF cuts, at three times the 375 px figure (1125 px wide PNGs).
`chartKey(chart, edition)` (the spec, the Haul's edition ink and `CHART_RENDER_VERSION`) keys each
pair; publish recomputes it from the chart it is publishing and refuses pictures drawn from anything
else, or of the wrong size. A preview without pictures only notes it; a publish without them stops.
The pictures are written next to the bundle (`hauls/NNN/media/<item>-<n>-chart-light.png`).

The Desk's CSP gains `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` for this: it allows compiling WebAssembly, and nothing else
(no string eval, no inline script). Tested in headless Chrome under the exact CSP: with it, the Desk's
code draws both sample charts in 1.3 s; without it, the browser refuses the wasm.

In the app, the title, note, units line, glosses, credit and table are native text that follows the
reader's text size; only the plot is a picture. Pictures are downloaded with the bundle and kept on
the phone (`hauls__…-chart-light.png`), so charts work offline. A chart without pictures (the dev
samples, an older publish) shows its table open with a quiet line.

When the renderer changes how a chart looks, bump `CHART_RENDER_VERSION` in `key.ts` so the next
publish redraws the pictures.

## Cost on the website

The website draws charts on every uncached render. After the first fixes the heaviest comps chart (a
ridgeline of 2,666 values) takes about 15 ms cold for both widths; most take 2 to 8 ms; a drawn chart
is kept per isolate (the bundle is already in memory), so later pages cost nothing extra. Warm chart
pages on the local Worker answer in about 10 ms, the same as other pages. The Worker grows from 202 to
301 KiB gzipped. See the report on the free plan's 10 ms limit.

## Changing the kit

- A new form: add it to `CHART_FORMS` and `FORMS`, write `forms/<form>.ts`, register it in
  `render.ts`, add a real-data fixture to `docs/design-comps/charts/data/` (the fixtures test checks
  every one) and a test.
- New fonts or cuts: run `python3 scripts/chart-metrics.py` (fonttools) to regenerate the metrics.
- Comps: `node_modules/.bin/esbuild docs/design-comps/charts/build-comps.ts --bundle --platform=node
  --format=esm --outfile=/tmp/build-comps.mjs && node /tmp/build-comps.mjs`, then
  `node docs/design-comps/_shared/shoot.mjs docs/design-comps/charts docs/design-comps/charts/shots 390,1280`.
- A quick look at one chart: `npm run editorial -- chart-draw DIR --data chart.json`.
