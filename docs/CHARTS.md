# Charts

The chart kit, built 2026-10-25 and refined 2026-10-26 (briefs: `docs/CHARTS-BRIEF.md`,
`docs/CHARTS-REFINE-BRIEF.md`; report: `docs/CHARTS-REPORT.md`). A chart is a block on an item, like
a pull quote or a picture. The editor brings the numbers and writes the title and the note; code draws
the chart in the house style, the same on the website and in the app, light and dark. A chart-led item
has its own content type, **Chart**, printed as **the figure**.

Everything a reader sees on a chart traces to rows stored in the bundle. The website draws the SVG
from those rows on every render. The app shows two pictures (light and dark) drawn from the same
scene, because it has no SVG runtime. Each chart also gets a **share picture**: the chart whole, with
its title, credit and the masthead, for social posts and Substack. The rows also print as a table
under every chart.

These are static charts, read at a glance on a phone and shared as pictures: every chart carries its
whole story without hover (direct labels, the key values written on it, annotations where the story
is), and is designed for the 375 px phone figure first.

Comps with real data: `docs/design-comps/charts/` (`time.html`, `compare.html`, `spread.html`,
`weather.html`, `places.html`, `money.html`, and `cards.html` for the share pictures; screenshots in
`shots/`, light and dark, 390 and 1280 wide; last night's first version in `shots/before/`; the Desk's
chart editor in `shots/desk/`).

## The forms

Twenty-one forms. Each draws at two widths: the **wide** figure (720 px, the reading column and some
of the air, from 560 px of column) and the **phone** figure (375 px, which is also the app's picture).
A container query on the figure's own width picks which one shows; both are in the page.

| Form | For | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `line` | change over time | Lines labelled at their ends, no legend. One loud line in the edition ink among quiet grey ones (`options.focus`), or up to six in the categorical order. `lo`/`hi` draw the uncertainty as a wash. A missing year breaks the line. |
| `multiples` | one line per place or group | A grid of panels on one shared scale, every other facet faint behind each panel, the focus facet in ink. Four across wide, two on a phone. |
| `area` | parts of a whole over time | Stacked from zero, 2 px ground gaps between layers. With a focus the story's parts are in the edition ink and the rest in three greys (neighbours always differ); else categorical. A part is named inside its band only where the whole word fits inside the band at every point under it; otherwise beside the plot on a leader. At most six parts. A missing value is refused. |
| `bar` | amounts | Horizontal bars for categories (`label`), columns for years (`x`). Square ends, at most 24 px thick, always from an ink zero line on the tightest round axis. Values at the tips (with halos). A focus (a category, or a column's year) is loud and the rest quiet grey. `lo`/`hi` draw whiskers; `n` under 30 draws hollow. |
| `dot` | values where zero means nothing | Cleveland dot plot with a hairline guide per row; several series get a key; whiskers for `lo`/`hi`; hollow for small samples. |
| `dumbbell` | two values per category | The first value a ring, the second a solid dot, so they read apart without a second hue. |
| `slope` | change between two moments | Two posts headed by the column names; labels spread so they never touch. When three or more values coincide at a post they share one bracket and one stub instead of a fan of crossing leaders. |
| `scatter` | two measures together | Dots ringed in the ground colour; `size` is dot area; every named point labelled, beside its dot when a spot is free, else further out on a leader, never dropped; `scale.x.log`/`scale.y.log` labelled. |
| `bump` | rankings over time | Ranks worked out by code in each period among the series shown (ties broken by name), names at both ends. |
| `stripes` | one value a year (Ed Hawkins's warming stripes) | Each year its own shade of a 41-step continuous diverging ramp, symmetric around the stated midpoint, edge to edge with no seams; a smooth key. |
| `band` | a year of daily weather | After the New York Times weather chart: quiet grey record and normal ranges, a fine bar for each day in the edition ink (half a day wide, so the normal shows between the days), record days marked, the year's highest and lowest named, a key drawn as one sample day. Built from daily data by `bandSummary` (below). |
| `calendar` | a value for every day | Weeks as columns, days as rows, months named; a missing day is an empty outlined square. Best as a departure from normal on the diverging ramp. |
| `heatmap` | a value for each pair of categories | Month by year, say; 2 px gaps; missing cells outlined; a ramp key. On a phone a long, short table turns on its side (years down the page). `options.values` prints every value in its cell, or none (a calendar of monthly returns). |
| `ridgeline` | how a distribution shifts across groups | A Gaussian kernel density per group (Silverman's bandwidth), one shared height scale, a median tick, every value marked for groups under 30. |
| `beeswarm` | every value as a dot | Deterministic packing, groups as rows (named in a left column on the wide figure, over each swarm on a phone), each group's median marked, named dots labelled with leaders where needed. |
| `waffle` | parts of a whole (instead of a pie) | 10 × 10 squares by largest-remainder rounding (or `options.per`). With a focus: the story's part in the edition ink, the rest a grey each when three or fewer, else one grey (the list names each part). Past five parts the smallest fold into "Other". |
| `histogram` | how many fall in each band of a value | Round bins (1, 2, 2.5 or 5 times a power of ten, 6 to 30 bars), bars on zero; `options.of` says what is counted ("countries"); named rows (focus) are loud and written over their bars with their values; rules mark thresholds. |
| `tilemap` | a value for each Indian state and union territory | Thirty-six equal tiles laid out roughly as the map (`geo/india.ts`), each with its code and value; older names accepted (Orissa, Pondicherry, Uttaranchal); a tile without data outlined and named in a gloss; the focus outlined and named in full. Draws no boundaries. |
| `choropleth` | a value for each country | Natural Earth's outlines in India's point of view (India's boundaries as India depicts them), Equal Earth projection (areas true), Antarctica left out; countries matched by ISO code or by the names the World Bank and OWID use; too-small countries as dots; no data in a quiet grey; the focus outlined and named. |
| `drawdown` | a price or index, and how far it fell | The price above (on a log scale when asked) and the drawdown below (how far below its highest point up to then). The deepest fall is shaded through both panels from peak to recovery, its low marked, and a gloss says how deep, from when to when, and when the price got back. |
| `table` | a few rows on their first and last values | First, last and change (a percentage for amounts, a difference for rates, shares, anomalies and indexes), and each row's path as a sparkline drawn to its own scale with its ends dotted; at most 25 rows; the focus row set in medium with its line in the edition ink. |

There are no pies, donuts, 3D charts or dual-axis charts; the validator refuses each by name and says
what to use instead. Not built, judged not to earn a place yet: a timeline of events (no openly
licensed events data for a real comp; periods on a line and the fingerprint cover much of it), a
population pyramid, a treemap and a Sankey (the waffle and the stacked area cover parts), a connected
scatter (the slope and the dumbbell cover before and after), a fan chart (a line's `lo`/`hi` wash
draws one band).

**Annotations** are a layer of every form that has room for them: `point` (a callout tied to a row,
placed in one of sixteen spots at two distances, clear of every mark and label, with a leader from its
ring), `period` (a shaded span with its name) and `rule` (a reference line with its label). The
editor's annotations are set in the italic serif; a machine annotation would be in the blue sans and
is checked (below).

## Labels never collide

Every form measures its words with the faces' real advance widths (`metrics.ts`) and places them
through `labels.ts`: a placer that knows what is already on the figure, tried against candidate spots
in order; a point's label goes beside its mark when a spot is free, else further out on a leader in
rings of growing distance, and only when nothing is free within reach is it left to the table. Labels
over gridlines or quiet marks wear a halo of the ground (SVG `paint-order: stroke`, a presentation
attribute, so the CSP is untouched). Labels inside fills take the text colour (white, near-black, ink
or ground) with the best worst-case contrast across both themes and every edition ink (`labelOn`).

`labels.test.ts` renders every comps fixture at both widths and its share card and fails on any two
labels that overlap, any label off its figure, or any name the editor made loud that is not written.

## Design

Typography carries it, as everywhere in Salvage. Around the plot, on the website and in the app:

- **Figure n** in quiet sans with the number in ember, then the editor's **title** in Newsreader
  Display and the **note** in the italic serif.
- The **head line** in quiet sans: what the numbers are, then where and when, in one line
  (`headLine`: "°C, difference from the 1961–1990 average; World, 1850 to 2025"). A whole year reads as
  the year ("2024", not "1 January 2024 to 31 December 2024").
- The plot.
- The machine's **glosses** in the blue sans (`chartGlosses`): the window shown against the source's
  span, log scales, a scale that does not start at zero, missing values, what the whiskers or the wash
  are, small samples, the quiet lines, how ranks, squares, bins or drawdowns were worked out, the gaps
  on a tile map, and the `method` of derived rows.
- The **credit**, one clean line (`creditParts`): each maker once with its datasets, "via" the
  redistributor, each licence once ("both CC BY 4.0" when they match), the day it was fetched. Every
  maker, dataset and redistributor is a link.
- **Download the chart as a picture** (the share picture) and **The numbers** (the data table).

Inside the plot: hairline gridlines in the rule colour, tick labels in ink2 sans in the left margin
with the unit on the top tick, an ink line at zero, square-ended marks, 2 px ground gaps between
touching marks and a 2 px ground ring on dots. Text never wears a data colour. One loud thing per
chart: the focus in the **Haul's edition ink**, everything else quiet. Ember is never data (it marks
only the brand on a share picture). Machine blue is never data.

### Colour

Neutral first: greys and ink carry the context, the Haul's edition ink marks the story, and a second
colour comes in only when a comparison needs it (two hemispheres, say). Warm colours mean heat: the
heat ramp is for temperatures only, and a temperature's departure from a normal takes the diverging
ramp, blue for cooler and red for warmer. The ramps are generated in OKLCH by `scripts/chart-ramps.mjs`
(even lightness steps) and each was run through the dataviz palette validator on this theme's ground,
2026-10-26:

| Ramp | Light | Dark | Validator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Categorical (teal, crimson, ochre, plum, green, steel) | `#008A7A #A4313F #C38323 #914A8C #518B44 #227CB1` | `#00A89F #C54F59 #BA8B25 #B167AB #639E56 #388EC4` | Unchanged from 2026-10-25: all checks pass in both themes. |
| Sequential, 7 steps, one cool hue (the default for magnitude) | `#8EBED4 … #002E59` | `#305275 … #BCE9FE` | Ordinal checks pass in both: lightness monotone, adjacent ΔL ≥ 0.06, one hue (spread 24° light, 22° dark), lightest step 2.01:1 on white and darkest 2.36:1 on the dark ground. |
| Heat, 7 steps (temperatures only) | `#EAAA6B … #6E0104` | `#972620 … #FFD5B0` | Pass in both: monotone, ΔL ≥ 0.06, hue spread 37° and 35°, end steps 2.01:1 and 2.38:1. |
| Diverging, 11 steps (blue, grey middle, red) | `#024077 … #ECEBE9 … #7D0008` | `#B0D5FE … #333331 … #FEBFB7` | Each arm passes on its own (monotone, ΔL ≥ 0.06, one hue, first tint 2.04 and 2.10:1 light, 2.81 and 2.65:1 dark). Matching steps of the two arms pass colour-blind separation (worst protan ΔE 12.3, light) and the normal-vision floor (worst 16.6). The arms are equal in lightness step for step, so a departure looks as strong either side. |
| Fine diverging, 41 steps (stripes) | interpolated in OKLab between the 11 steps above | | Every fourth step is one of the validated steps. |
| Greys and bands | quiet `#C2C4C8`, quiet2 `#DDDEE1`, quiet3 `#96989D`; record `#ECEDEF`, normal `#D1D2D6` | quiet `#4C4D50`, quiet2 `#343539`, quiet3 `#707175`; record `#222325`, normal `#38393C` | Neutral (no hue to speak of). |

Ramp binning (`ramp.ts`), stated so a reader can check any cell: sequential bins are equal and round
(the top bin of a share in per cent ends at 100); diverging bins are centred on the midpoint, sized so
95 per cent of the values sit in the nine inner bins, and when the rest reach further the two end bins
are open and drawn as points in the key (as an atlas does), so one outlier never flattens the rest.

## The data spec

A `Chart` (`types.ts`) is JSON:

```json
{
  "form": "line",
  "title": "The world has warmed by about one and a half degrees",
  "note": "The shaded band is the 95 per cent range of the estimate.",
  "units": "°C",
  "geography": "World",
  "span": { "from": "1850", "to": "2025" },
  "sourceSpan": { "from": "1850", "to": "2026" },
  "sources": [{
    "maker": "Met Office Hadley Centre", "dataset": "HadCRUT5",
    "url": "https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/hadobs/hadcrut5/",
    "licence": "Open Government Licence v3", "retrieved": "2026-10-25",
    "via": { "name": "Our World in Data", "url": "https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/temperature-anomaly", "licence": "CC BY 4.0" }
  }],
  "measure": { "basis": "anomaly", "base": "the 1861–1890 average" },
  "data": {
    "columns": [
      { "key": "year", "label": "Year", "type": "year" },
      { "key": "anomaly", "label": "Global average temperature anomaly", "type": "number" },
      { "key": "lower", "label": "Lower bound (95% confidence)", "type": "number" },
      { "key": "upper", "label": "Upper bound (95% confidence)", "type": "number" }
    ],
    "rows": [[1850, -0.05, -0.23, 0.13], [1851, 0.1, -0.08, 0.28]]
  },
  "encode": { "x": "year", "y": "anomaly", "lo": "lower", "hi": "upper" },
  "options": { "focus": [] },
  "annotations": [{ "kind": "period", "from": 1914, "to": 1918, "text": "First World War", "by": "editor" }]
}
```

- **Columns** are `number`, `text`, `year` (a whole number) or `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD` or `YYYY-MM`). A
  missing value is `null`, never text or NaN.
- **Encode** maps roles to columns: `x`, `y`, `series`, `label`, `facet`, `lo`, `hi`, `n`, `size`,
  `from`, `to`, the band's `normalLo`, `normalHi`, `recordLo`, `recordHi`, and `code` (an ISO code, for
  matching places on a map). `FORMS[form]` lists each form's required and optional roles and the column
  types each role takes.
- **Measure** says what the numbers are: `absolute`, `per-capita`, `share` or `rate` (with a
  `denominator`), `index`, `anomaly` or `change` (with a `base`), and `money` (`currency`, `nominal` or
  `real`, `baseYear` for real).
- **Options**: `colour` (`focus`, `categorical`, `sequential`, `heat`, `diverging`; each form allows
  some), `focus` (names to set loud; on columns, years), `midpoint` (`{ value, label }`, diverging
  only), `sort` (`value` or `given`), `per` (waffle), `rank` (bump), `of` (histogram: what is counted),
  `values` (heatmap: print the values).
- **Scale**: `y.log`, `x.log` (scatter), `y.zero`.
- **method**: one plain sentence when code derived the rows (a band's normals, departures, monthly
  changes). It prints as a gloss.

In a bundle a chart is `{ "kind": "chart", "chart": { … }, "images": { "light": {url, width, height}, "dark": {…}, "share": {…} } }`.
`share` is new (2026-10-26); bundles without it still validate.

## The validator's rules

`validateChart(chart)` runs on the Desk before a chart can be kept (and live in the editor), again at
publish, and inside `validateHaul` before a bundle renders. Each rule has a test in
`packages/shared/src/charts/validate.test.ts` or `forms/forms-d.test.ts`; the comps fixtures must pass
it too.

| Rule | What it requires |
|---|---|
| `title`, `units`, `geography` | A title; what the numbers are measured in; where the data is about, or `null` for no place. |
| `source`, `licence` | At least one source with maker, dataset, an https link, the retrieved date, and the licence as the institution states it ("unknown" and empty are refused). A redistributor (`via`) needs its own licence. |
| `zero-baseline`, `log` | Bars, areas, waffles and histograms start at zero and are never on a log scale. A line may leave zero out; a gloss then says so. A log scale needs every value above zero, only on lines, dots, scatters and prices, and is always labelled. |
| `dual-axis`, `3d`, `pie`, `colour`, `unknown-field` | No second y axis, no 3D, no pies (use a waffle or bars), no home-made palettes or rainbows. Any field the spec does not know is refused, so nothing unchecked reaches a bundle. |
| `colour`, `too-many-series` | Ordered values never get separate hues. Categorical colour is capped at six series (twelve parts for a waffle, which folds the rest). |
| `heat` | The warm heat ramp only for temperatures (°C, °F, kelvin); a departure from a baseline takes the diverging ramp. |
| `midpoint` | A diverging ramp needs a meaningful midpoint with its name; a midpoint outside the data is warned. |
| `basis`, `denominator`, `base`, `index-base`, `money` | Per-capita or absolute is stated; shares and rates show their denominator; an index states its base and the series must read that level in that year; anomalies and changes state their base; money states its currency and nominal or real, real money its base year; nominal money over ten years or more is warned. |
| `uncertainty`, `sample-size` | If the data carries uncertainty or sample sizes, they must be encoded and shown, or the column removed. Samples under 30 are drawn hollow. |
| `missing` | Missing values are `null` and drawn as missing; absent years in a yearly series are found and drawn as gaps too. A stacked area with a hole is refused. |
| `span` | A chart over time records the span it shows (exactly the rows' extent) and the full span of the source; a narrower window gets a gloss. |
| `data`, `size`, `duplicate`, `encode` | Every cell matches its column type; at most 20,000 rows, 80,000 cells and 1.5 MB; malformed tables are refused; no two points at the same place (including two rows for one state or country); every role points at a column of the right type. |
| `histogram` | A histogram says what each bar counts (`options.of`). |
| `tilemap` | Every row names one of India's states or union territories (current names or older ones). |
| `choropleth` | Rows with no shape on the map (regions, income groups, Kosovo in India's view) are warned and stay in the table. |
| `drawdown` | Prices or index levels above zero. |
| `table` | At most 25 rows. |
| `figures` | Every figure in a **machine** annotation must be a number in the rows, a year in them, or a number in the editor's own stated metadata. Fail-closed. |
| `style:*` | Machine annotations, glosses, the method and the text alternative pass `checkStyle`. |

What code writes from the rows (glosses, the description, ranks, squares, bins, densities, drawdowns,
band normals, table changes) is computed, not drafted, and is tested; no model writes any number on a
chart.

## Getting the data in

### A table (CSV or TSV)

Paste or upload on the Desk, or `npm run editorial -- chart-csv FILE`. `csvToData` reads the header,
types each column, reads thousands commas and any minus sign, and treats blank, `NA`, `n/a`, `..`, `-`
as missing. A cell is a number only when the whole cell is one. The editor states the source, its
licence and the source's full span.

### Our World in Data (checked 2026-10-25)

- Endpoints (Chart API, docs.owid.io/projects/etl/api/chart-api/, "License: CC BY 4.0"):
  `https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/<slug>.csv?v=1&csvType=full&useColumnShortNames=true` and
  `<slug>.metadata.json`.
- Licences: each indicator's `https://api.ourworldindata.org/v1/indicators/<id>.metadata.json` lists its
  `origins` with `producer`, `title`, `urlMain` and `license { name, url }`. The adapter credits each
  producer with its own licence and Our World in Data as the redistributor. An origin without a stated
  licence is refused.
- A year at the end of OWID's span can be partial; check the latest year before keeping a chart.

### World Bank (checked 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-26)

- API v2: `https://api.worldbank.org/v2/indicator/<id>?format=json`, then
  `https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/<ISO;ISO>/indicator/<id>?format=json&per_page=20000`. The
  `countryiso3code` of each row is the map's `code`.
- Licence: `https://api.worldbank.org/v2/sources/<source>/series/<id>/metadata?format=json` carries
  `License_Type` ("CC BY-4.0" for World Development Indicators). A series without one is refused.
- The Pink Sheet (commodity prices, monthly and annual, nominal and real 2010 US$): the Commodity
  Markets page (worldbank.org/en/research/commodity-markets) gives the files and points "Using this
  Data" to the CC BY licence.
- Indian states (2011-era indicators, CC BY 4.0): the India sub-national dataset
  (datacatalog.worldbank.org, dataset 0041418). More recent state data sits on data.gov.in (Government
  Open Data License, India) behind a keyed API, or with the RBI, whose terms are not an open licence;
  use the table route with the licence as stated.

### Other sources

Any source works through the table route, as long as its licence is stated. The comps use Open-Meteo's
historical API (ERA5 reanalysis, CC BY 4.0) for Delhi's daily temperatures and the Met Office's own
HadCRUT5 series (Open Government Licence v3) for the warming stripes. See
`docs/design-comps/charts/raw/SOURCES-*.md`.

### Maps

The world map's outlines are Natural Earth's (public domain) Admin 0 countries in **India's point of
view**: Jammu and Kashmir (with Gilgit-Baltistan and Aksai Chin) and Arunachal Pradesh drawn within
India, as India depicts its boundaries; the default Natural Earth file draws the de facto lines, and
an Indian publication should not. A test checks Gilgit, Aksai Chin and Tawang fall inside India and
not inside Pakistan or China. `scripts/chart-world.py` projects them (Equal Earth), simplifies to
about a quarter of a pixel at 720 px and writes `geo/world.ts` (247 countries, 42 kB gzipped). The
India tile grid draws no boundaries at all.

### A temperature band from daily data

`bandSummary(daily, { year, normalFrom, normalTo })` turns daily highs and lows into the band's rows:
that year's low and high each day, the normal range (mean daily low and high on that calendar day over
the normal years), and the records up to and including the year. `bandMethod` writes the method.

### Someone else's chart, as it stands

A **picture block**, under the picture credit rule: maker, holder, licence as stated, a link back;
fetched through `guardedFetch` with a byte cap, kept in MEDIA and copied to FILES at publish, never
hotlinked. Redrawing from the data is better whenever the licence allows.

## How the editor adds a chart

### On the Desk

1. On the item, choose **Chart** under Add.
2. Bring the numbers: paste a table or upload a CSV; paste an Our World in Data link; or give a World
   Bank indicator code and country codes.
3. Pick a form (only the forms the columns can feed are offered), check which column plays which role.
4. Write the title and the note in your own words, the units, and the place.
5. Say what the numbers are, and for a table the source, its licence and the source's full span.
6. Optional: what is loud, colour, midpoint, order, log, what a histogram counts, values in a heatmap's
   cells, callouts, periods and lines.
7. The preview redraws as you go (the wide figure and the phone figure), with the head line, the glosses
   and the validator's errors and warnings. **Keep** is off until the errors are gone.
8. **Download the picture** draws the share picture on the Desk Worker and saves it, before or without
   publishing.
9. At **Publish**, the Desk Worker draws each chart's app pictures and share picture and writes them
   next to the bundle.

### From the terminal (or an agent)

```sh
npm run editorial -- chart-fetch --data source.json      # {"provider":"owid","chart":URL,"entities":["India","World"]}
npm run editorial -- chart-csv rainfall.csv              # a table as chart data (local)
npm run editorial -- chart-check --data chart.json       # the validator, units line, glosses (local)
npm run editorial -- chart-draw /tmp/look --data chart.json   # PNGs to look at, and the share picture (local)
```

## The pictures (app and share)

The app has no SVG runtime and stays lean, so it shows pictures of the phone figure, light and dark,
1125 px wide (three times the 375 px figure). Each chart also has a **share picture**, 1080 px wide:
the Haul's ink along the top, the ember flame and the struck masthead, the title, the note, the head
line, the phone figure, the glosses, the credit and the chart's address.

All three are drawn **on the server at publish** by the Desk Worker (`packages/server/src/chartdraw.ts`)
with resvg in WebAssembly, bundled by wrangler as a compiled module (`workers/api/src/draw.ts`), and the
app's own font cuts read from the Desk's assets. Publish draws from the chart it is publishing, so no
picture can come from anything else, and names each file with the chart's key
(`hauls/NNN/media/<item>-<n>-chart-<key>-light.png`): files under `hauls/` are cached as immutable, so
a changed chart gets a new address. The Desk compiles no WebAssembly (its CSP is `script-src 'self'`).

Measured (2026-10-26, `scripts/chart-raster-bench.ts`): the wasm is 2.48 MB, 0.96 MB gzipped, and
starts in 0.2 s; each comps chart's two app pictures take 0.1 to 0.7 s of CPU (all nineteen first
charts: 4.6 s). The Desk Worker bundles to 1.85 MB gzipped. Publishing a calendar chart on a local
workerd, drawing included, took 1.2 s; a share picture of the world map, 0.6 s. Workers Paid allows 30
seconds of CPU per request by default and 10 MB per Worker.

In the app, the title, note, head line, glosses, credit and table are native text that follows the
reader's text size; only the plot is a picture, kept on the phone with the bundle so charts work
offline. "Share the chart as a picture" shares the share picture's address. A chart without pictures
shows its table open with a quiet line.

When the renderer changes how a chart looks, bump `CHART_RENDER_VERSION` in `key.ts` so the next
publish draws new pictures under new names.

## Cost on the website

The website draws charts on every uncached render; a drawn chart is kept per isolate. The heaviest
comps chart takes about 15 ms cold for both widths. The site is on Workers Paid, so the free plan's
10 ms limit no longer applies.

## Changing the kit

- A new form: add it to `CHART_FORMS` and `FORMS`, write `forms/<form>.ts`, register it in `render.ts`,
  add a real-data fixture to `docs/design-comps/charts/data/` (the fixtures, labels and share-card tests
  check every one) and its own tests.
- Colours: edit `scripts/chart-ramps.mjs`, run it, paste into `chartInk`, and run each ramp through the
  dataviz validator (each diverging arm on its own; matching arm steps as pairs).
- New fonts or cuts: `python3 scripts/chart-metrics.py` (fonttools). New marks: `python3 scripts/chart-marks.py`.
  New map outlines: `python3 scripts/chart-world.py <ne_10m_admin_0_countries_ind.geojson>`.
- Comps: `node_modules/.bin/esbuild docs/design-comps/charts/build-comps.ts --bundle --platform=node
  --format=esm --outfile=/tmp/build-comps.mjs && node /tmp/build-comps.mjs`, then
  `node docs/design-comps/_shared/shoot.mjs docs/design-comps/charts docs/design-comps/charts/shots 390,1280`.
  Fixture builders: `raw/build-A.py`, `raw/build-C.py`, `raw/build-D.py`, `build-b.ts`.
- A quick look at one chart: `scripts/chart-preview.ts` (a contact sheet of both widths and themes, or
  `--share` for the share picture), or `npm run editorial -- chart-draw DIR --data chart.json`.
- The dev sample Hauls' pictures: `scripts/chart-seed-pictures.ts`.
